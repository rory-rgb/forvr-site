Process

How a project runs.

Two things worth separating: how working together goes, and how the making itself goes. Both are below, and the second one is shown rather than described.

Part one · Working together

Five stages, every job

01 Discovery

One call

What you want, what the business needs, what done looks like.

Out: a one-page brief

02 Research

Line it up

References, competitors, the brand itself, read against the objectives.

Out: the direction

03 System

Build the world

A visual system tailored to the client, nothing off the shelf.

Out: the system, signed off

04 Production

Make the work

Film, stills, identity, documents, whichever the job calls for.

Out: rounds you react to

05 Delivery

Hand it over

Every asset in every format it needs to live in.

Out: the files and a debrief

The weight of each stage shifts with the job. A film leans on 04, an identity on 03. None of them skip 01.

Part two · Inside the making

Statue of a Fool, in full →

Stage 04 above is where most studios go quiet. Here is what actually happens inside it, run against Statue of a Fool, a collection I made with nobody commissioning it.

Codex, page one

Luxury always undercut by the mundane. Never fully clean, never complete glam. Identity half hidden.

01 Direction

Write the world first

Before anything is made, I write a codex. It fixes the palette, the quality of light, the wardrobe and the two tonal poles the collection swings between. For this one the poles were marble and dirt: classical posture on one side, worn everyday clothing on the other.

The codex is short and specific enough to settle arguments later. Every stage below is checked against it.

Out: the codex, palette and wardrobe fixed

02 Graphic

Build the statement artwork

The collection needed one piece of artwork strong enough to carry the whole thing: a veiled marble figure standing inside display type filled with banknotes. Both poles of the codex in one image. I worked it until it read at thumbnail size, because that is where most people meet it.

Everything after this point is downstream of the graphic.

Out: master artwork, print-ready

03 Garment

Put it on a real product

Artwork on a screen proves nothing. The print goes onto a washed black hooded sweatshirt with studded cuffs, then gets distressed so it sits in the fabric rather than floating on top of it. Placement, scale and wear all get resolved here.

This is usually where a nice graphic turns out to be the wrong graphic. Better to find that now.

Out: the lead piece, front and back

04 Collection

Extend without diluting

The hood becomes a small collection: a thermal longsleeve with engraved filigree, a jersey, denim jorts with contrast stitching, each in two colourways. Different pieces, one world, held together by the wash and the palette rather than by repeating the same print.

Restraint matters more than range. Four pieces that agree with each other beat twelve that do not.

Out: the range, colourways locked

05 Campaign

Shoot the myth

The campaign puts the collection on a cast of faces across London after dark: estate courtyards, a chicken shop counter, the off-licence fridge, a laundrette, a bus stop bench. Hard flash, wet roads, sodium light. The marble stays in the graphic, never in the photography.

Holding one cast, one light and one grade across a dozen locations is the hard part of AI-led imagery. It is where most of the time goes.

Out: the campaign set, one grade across every frame

06 Delivery

Hand over everything

Masters, crops for each placement, and the macro detail shots that do the selling on a product page. Files named so you can find them in six months without asking me.

The codex goes with the files. If you take the brand elsewhere later, the direction travels with it.

Out: every asset, every crop, plus the codex
