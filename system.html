Read firstThe trap

The reason AI built sites all look the same is the reference set, not the model.

One of the biggest selling libraries of website prompts is roughly 70 percent dark purple particle fields, galaxies, orbs, nebulas and gradient blobs. It moves at volume, so those looks are now the default. Feed one in and you get everyone else's homepage back, which is why you can spot one at a glance.

This matters more here than anywhere, because a dark page with a glowing 3D object in the middle of it is exactly where that default lives. PYRE is standing right next to it.

It gets away with it for one reason, and the whole of this sheet is really about that one reason: the glow is a measurement, not a mood.

01The mechanic

Start with something true, not something that looks good

The best sites have one idea that is genuinely true about the product and is also the thing you operate.

Heat a piece of metal and it glows dull red, then orange, then amber, then white. That is not a metaphor, it is black body radiation, and the colour maps to a real temperature in Kelvin. Chilli heat has a scale too, Scoville.

So PYRE maps one onto the other. Every sauce's colour is the temperature it burns at. Nothing on the page is coloured because it looked nice.

SauceScovilleSits at
Ember1,200Dull red, barely lit
Forge15,000Red going orange
Kiln85,000Full orange
Crucible400,000Amber, close to yellow
White1,600,000White hot

The whole page then runs on one number between 0 and 1. Scrolling drives it up. As it climbs, the background burns from cold blue-white to white hot, the type gets physically wider rather than just bolder, the film grain coarsens, and a heat haze starts rising over the bottle. At 66 percent the text flips from light to dark, because white type on a white hot screen disappears.

That same number has a second input. Hovering a bottle further down the page drives it to that sauce's value and repaints the entire viewport in under half a second. One machine, two inputs. It is not a hover state on a card, it is the room changing temperature.

The test that kills most designs

Could this design be lifted onto a competitor with a logo swap?

If yes, you have decorated a product rather than designed for one. PYRE's colour system is meaningless on a brand that does not sell measured heat, and that is the point of it.

Answer this before you write a single prompt: what is true about this product that I could let someone operate? If you cannot answer it, you do not have a site yet, you have a skin.

02The references

Give it real URLs, and say what to take from each

"Make it look premium" returns the average of everything it has ever seen. That average is the problem.

These are real sites, live at the time of writing. The second column is the useful half. Handing over a reference without saying what to take from it is how you end up with a copy of the weakest thing on the page.

SiteTake thisDo not take
ciaoenergy.com The closest thing to what PYRE does. A product object you can turn, a colour system where picking a variant repaints the whole page, one loud type moment, and a hard flip into light mode. To find your own

Galleries are worth mining for outbound links, not for copying. Pull the real site URLs out of a category index, then go and look at the actual sites. One index gives you twenty candidates.

  • recent.design, minimal.gallery, httpster.net, land-book.com and siteinspire.com all list real, live, outbound work.
  • awwwards.com and dribbble.com are worth searching to learn which names to look up. They are not worth copying from. Half of Dribbble was never built.

Three instructions that change the output

  1. Ask for the real tokens, not an impression. Have it fetch the live page and pull the actual hex values, font families and heading and body sizes. An impression of a colour is a slightly wrong colour, and a page full of slightly wrong colours is exactly what generic looks like.
  2. Score the field, then pick a fight. Have it score five real competitors on six or seven axes, one to ten, from the live sites rather than from reputation. Then find the axis where the whole field is weak and win that one. That single gap is your site.
  3. Ask what it should not copy. Every reference has one thing worth stealing and one thing that would sink you. Naming both is what stops it blending them into mush. This is the cheapest and most underused instruction here.
Do not hand it any of these

Prompt libraries as a look reference. Dashboard shots that only ever existed as a picture. Any brief containing the words sleek, modern or cutting edge, which mean nothing and get averaged into nothing.

And a hard stop: the moment you see a purple particle field, a glowing orb, a wireframe head or a glassmorphic card, you have arrived at the default. Start again.

03The camera

If the point is a reel, the site is not the deliverable

A phone video of a monitor is the deliverable. Design for the second generation image, not the screenshot.

  • Saturated colour and bloom survive being re-filmed. Thin grey type and subtle gradients turn to mud. PYRE has no greys in its palette at all. Everything is ink, ground, or a point on the curve.
  • The payoff lands inside the first two seconds. There is no patient viewer, so nothing is held back for one. PYRE's colour curve is deliberately front loaded so the heat arrives fast and the long tail is the climb to white.
  • Compose everything that matters into the middle 60 percent of the viewport. The edges get cropped or lost to glare.
  • One loud type moment. Enormous display against very small body copy is the ratio the good sites all share.
  • Measure contrast, do not eyeball it, and measure it at every value of your driving number rather than only at the ends. A page that reheats has to stay readable the whole way up.

Prefer geometry to frames

PYRE's bottle is real 3D geometry, not a photo and not a rendered image sequence. A sauce bottle is a surface of revolution, so it is built by spinning a drawn profile: glass shell, a second inner shape for the liquid at a fill level, a cylinder for the label. It is smooth by definition, so it cannot stutter, and it weighs a fraction of a frame set.

The label is real type drawn onto a texture, never a generated image. That is the single most important asset decision on the page. Generated lettering is the most obvious AI tell there is, it goes soft under recapture, and real type recolours per sauce for free.

The one that catches almost everybody

If any part of the page is a scroll scrubbed image sequence, budget one frame for every 12 to 16 pixels of pinned scroll, with a floor of 90 frames.

Go below that and a slow, deliberate scroll changes the picture only a handful of times a second. Everyone who watches it will call it laggy and they will all be wrong about the cause, because the framerate is fine. Steppy reads as laggy. If the file size blows out, shorten the scroll distance. Never thin the frames.

Two that only bite once you are actually building

  • Interpolate colour in linear light. Blending two colours in normal sRGB values dips through mud in the middle, so a red to amber ramp goes brown halfway. Convert, blend, convert back.
  • Glowing materials clip toward yellow. Push a light emitting material toward pale cream and red saturates first, then green, and your white hot moment turns lime. The fix is to hold green under a ceiling at every stop on the curve rather than trusting the colour you picked.
04The prompt

Fill in the two brackets and paste it

Written for Claude Code, works anywhere that can read a URL and write files. It deliberately stops before it builds anything, because the first direction is never the one you want.

Then score five real competitors in my category on these axes, one
to ten, from the live sites and not from reputation: product
legibility, strength of the claim, motion craft, how well it

RULES
1. One value between 0 and 1 drives the entire page. Write it to CSS
   custom properties and to a ref inside one requestAnimationFrame
   loop. Damp it. No React state updates per frame anywhere.
2. Give that value two inputs, not one: scroll, and a direct
   interaction that overrides scroll. Same machine both times.
3. The colour system must be a real phenomenon that is also the
   product's claim, so the colours mean something. If a competitor
   could swap their logo onto this page and lose nothing, you have
   not designed anything. Tell me how the page fails that test.
4. The hero object is real 3D geometry, not a photograph and not a
   rendered image sequence. Any lettering on the product is real
   type drawn to a texture, never a generated image.
5. Interpolate every colour in linear light, not in sRGB.
6. Assert every text and background pair in code and print the
   contrast ratios, at several values of the driving number, not
   just at the ends. Under 4.5 to 1 is not allowed to be type.
7. Enormous display type against very small body copy. Hand break
   every heading. Nothing may reflow mid scroll.
8. Saturated colour, hard contrast, payoff inside two seconds, and
   everything that matters in the middle 60 percent of the
   viewport. This gets watched as a phone video of a monitor. No
   greys anywhere in the palette.
9. Any scroll scrubbed image sequence gets one frame per 12 to 16
   pixels of pinned scroll, minimum 90 frames. If that is too
   heavy, shorten the scroll. Never thin the frames.

BANNED
Purple or indigo particle fields, galaxies, orbs, nebulas, gradient
blobs, wireframe heads, glassmorphism, a hero that is a video loop
with type on top, and cards with a coloured bar down one edge. Also
banned: fade up on scroll as the only motion on the page. If the
only thing that happens when I scroll is that things appear, you
have not built anything.

STACK
Next.js app router, Tailwind, React Three Fiber for the object, GSAP
with ScrollTrigger, Lenis for smoothed scroll. Seven to nine
sections. Every interaction needs a reduced motion and a mobile
fallback, and the fallback keeps the information even where it
drops the motion.

HOW TO WORK
Stop before you build. Show me the mechanic in one sentence, the
scroll story as a table of beats, the palette with measured contrast
ratios, the type scale, and which reference each decision came from.
I will reject it. Expect to go around two or three times. Write no
page code until I say go.
05The revisions

Do not accept the first build

Rejecting is the job. Every good thing on PYRE arrived because the version before it was thrown out.

Three things that actually went wrong on this page, none of which a prompt would have prevented:

  • The hero bottle was wired to one sauce while the copy underneath named a different one, so the page quietly contradicted itself. Nobody spots that in a plan, only on screen.
  • At full heat the liquid went lime green. Pushing a glowing material toward pale cream saturates red first, then green, so the peak clipped to yellow.
  • Crossfading between two label textures produced double exposed, unreadable lettering. That is the exact thing that makes a page look machine made, so it was replaced with a hard swap hidden inside a brief dip in brightness.

These three follow ups do more than any amount of extra prompting up front:

  1. "Which parts of this page would look identical if it were selling something else? Rip those out and replace them with something only this product could do."
  2. "Show me the contrast ratios and the frame arithmetic as numbers, not as adjectives."
  3. "You have landed on a default. Name the three most predictable choices on this page and give me a real alternative for each."

The first one is the sharpest. Almost every generic section on almost every AI built page fails it.
