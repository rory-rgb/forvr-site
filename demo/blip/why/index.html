The Reading Room

It is a placebo.
That is the point.

Externalising a worry onto an object is a real, old and well documented technique. It does not cure anything. Here is exactly what we claim and exactly what we do not.

What we claim

That giving a feeling a name and a place outside the body makes it easier to carry. That is it. That is the whole claim. Play therapists have used dolls, puppets and worry objects for decades for exactly this reason, and worry dolls have existed for a great deal longer than that.

What we do not claim

We do not claim a BLIP treats anxiety. We do not claim it is clinically tested, because it is not. We do not claim any child needs one. And we are not going to put a scientist in a lab coat on this page and imply an endorsement that does not exist.

Why rarity is what it is

Rarity here is how common the feeling is, not how few we made. The dark is Common because most children have it. Missing somebody who is not coming back is Rare because thankfully most children do not, yet. We are never going to manufacture scarcity out of a child's worst week.

If a child is genuinely struggling

Talk to your GP or your child's school. In the UK, YoungMinds runs a parents helpline and it is free. A toy is a handle, not a treatment, and anyone selling you otherwise is selling you something else.
